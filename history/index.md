# History
Spiers-Dennis Post No. 24 has been part of Olathe for more than a century. Our Post was already active here by **1920**.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-coconino-sun-1920" aria-label="Reference 1">[1]</a></sup>
Our Post bears the names of two men connected to Olathe who lost their lives in military service during World War I: **Private Earl C. Spiers, U.S. Army**, and **Seaman John T. Dennis, U.S. Navy**.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-legion-centennial-post24" aria-label="Reference 2">[2]</a></sup>
## Namesakes
### Earl Christie Spiers, U.S. Army

Earl Christie Spiers was born in Olathe, Colorado, and served as a private in the **109th Infantry Regiment**, 55th Infantry Brigade, 28th Division, U.S. Army.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-colorado-freedom-spiers" aria-label="Reference 3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-pennsylvania-heritage-28th" aria-label="Reference 18">[18]</a></sup> The 28th Division was drawn principally from the Pennsylvania National Guard and became known as the **“Iron Division.”**<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-pennsylvania-heritage-28th" aria-label="Reference 18">[18]</a></sup> The 109th had already fought against the German offensive at the Marne in July 1918 and in the Allied advance that followed. These are documented events in Spiers's regiment, but the available records do not establish which of its earlier engagements he personally experienced.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-pennsylvania-heritage-28th" aria-label="Reference 18">[18]</a></sup><sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-proctor-iron-division" aria-label="Reference 19">[19]</a></sup>

In early August, the 28th Division returned to the front around **Fismes**, on the Vesle River in northeastern France. On August 13, the 109th relieved the 111th Infantry in this sector as the division's positions were reorganized.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-pennsylvania-heritage-28th" aria-label="Reference 18">[18]</a></sup> The Americans endured German machine-gun fire, artillery bombardment and gas attacks while holding positions along the river. Control of Fismes and its northern suburb, Fismette, was fiercely contested; German forces retook Fismette on August 27.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-army-oise-aisne-28th" aria-label="Reference 8">[8]</a></sup><sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-pennsylvania-heritage-28th" aria-label="Reference 18">[18]</a></sup>

During the night of **September 3–4, 1918**, German forces began withdrawing from much of their Vesle line as Allied attacks farther west threatened their positions. The 28th Division pursued on September 4, moving across the Vesle and onto the high ground between that river and the Aisne.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-army-oise-aisne-28th" aria-label="Reference 8">[8]</a></sup><sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-pennsylvania-heritage-28th" aria-label="Reference 18">[18]</a></sup> Elements of the **109th Infantry crossed from the Magneux sector, east of Fismes**, with Muscourt farther north among the regiment's objectives.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-proctor-iron-division" aria-label="Reference 19">[19]</a></sup><sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-asu-109th-vesle" aria-label="Reference 20">[20]</a></sup>

What initially appeared to be a pursuit soon became a costly fight. The 109th advanced through wooded slopes toward an exposed plateau, where German machine guns covered the approaches from higher ground. Patrols reported strong machine-gun positions across the regiment's front.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-asu-109th-vesle" aria-label="Reference 20">[20]</a></sup> An attack on **September 5** met concentrated fire when American troops reached the crest. The regiment's experience illustrates why the division's advance slowed despite the wider German withdrawal.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-asu-109th-vesle" aria-label="Reference 20">[20]</a></sup><sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-army-oise-aisne-28th" aria-label="Reference 8">[8]</a></sup> The neighboring American 77th Division advanced farther toward the Aisne on the left, while the 28th encountered especially strong resistance near Glennes.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-army-oise-aisne-28th" aria-label="Reference 8">[8]</a></sup>

On **September 6**, the fighting reached the command of Spiers's own regiment. **Colonel Samuel Vinton Ham**, commanding the 109th, was severely wounded while leading his firing line in an advance across the Vesle from Magneux toward Muscourt. According to his Distinguished Service Cross citation, he remained on the battlefield for ten hours, continued directing the attack and refused evacuation or medical attention until his men had been cared for.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-ham-distinguished-service-cross" aria-label="Reference 21">[21]</a></sup> That same day **Sergeant William R. Wallace of Company G, 109th Infantry**, was twice wounded by machine-gun bullets near Baslieux but continued leading his platoon through machine-gun and artillery fire.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-wallace-distinguished-service-cross" aria-label="Reference 22">[22]</a></sup> These documented actions reveal the dangers facing the regiment; neither establishes Spiers's company or his exact position.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-ham-distinguished-service-cross" aria-label="Reference 21">[21]</a></sup><sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-wallace-distinguished-service-cross" aria-label="Reference 22">[22]</a></sup>

**September 7, 1918, was the day Earl Christie Spiers died in France.**<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-colorado-freedom-spiers" aria-label="Reference 3">[3]</a></sup> The 28th Division was still confronting German resistance along the plateau. Army records describe machine-gun and artillery fire, German counterattacks and gas shells in the division's sector during these final days of its advance.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-army-oise-aisne-28th" aria-label="Reference 8">[8]</a></sup> The division had been ordered relieved by the French 62nd Division, a withdrawal completed during the night of September 7–8.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-pennsylvania-heritage-28th" aria-label="Reference 18">[18]</a></sup> The available divisional and regimental histories describe the fighting surrounding Spiers's recorded date of death, but **his precise location and the circumstances of his death have not been established**.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-colorado-freedom-spiers" aria-label="Reference 3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-army-oise-aisne-28th" aria-label="Reference 8">[8]</a></sup>

Spiers is buried at **Oise-Aisne American Cemetery**, France, **Plot D, Row 9, Grave 20**.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-spiers-oise-aisne" aria-label="Reference 4">[4]</a></sup>

### John Thomas Dennis — U.S. Navy
John Thomas Dennis was a seaman in the U.S. Navy whose home and family connections were in Olathe. He enlisted at Des Moines, Iowa, on November 11, 1916, and later served aboard **USS *Cyclops* (AC-4)**, a Navy collier.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-navy-war-dead-dennis" aria-label="Reference 5">[5]</a></sup><sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-navy-cyclops-assessment" aria-label="Reference 9">[9]</a></sup>
On her final voyage, *Cyclops* was carrying approximately 10,800 long tons of manganese ore from Brazil to Baltimore. Before leaving Brazil, the ship had developed a cracked cylinder in her starboard engine, leaving her to proceed under the power of the remaining engine. The heavy ore cargo also raised questions about the ship's stability and structural strength.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-naval-historical-foundation-cyclops" aria-label="Reference 10">[10]</a></sup>
On **March 4, 1918**, *Cyclops* left Barbados for Baltimore with 306 people aboard, including Dennis. She disappeared without a distress call. No one aboard is known to have survived, and no wreck has ever been found.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-navy-lost-all-hands-1918" aria-label="Reference 11">[11]</a></sup> Later weather research found a front across her estimated position on **March 8** and a severe Atlantic gale on March 9–10, which nearly sank another vessel, the freighter *Amalco*. Whether *Cyclops* encountered that storm is unknown.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-usni-cyclops-weather-1970" aria-label="Reference 13">[13]</a></sup> Navy historians have considered whether the dense cargo, engine trouble, heavy seas, and possible structural weaknesses contributed to her loss, but the cause remains unknown.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-navy-cyclops-assessment" aria-label="Reference 9">[9]</a></sup><sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-naval-historical-foundation-cyclops" aria-label="Reference 10">[10]</a></sup> The Navy officially recorded Dennis's death as June 14, 1918, although that was not a known date of sinking.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-navy-war-dead-dennis" aria-label="Reference 5">[5]</a></sup>
## Our first clubhouse
In the fall of **1921**, Post 24 was meeting in a rented house on Main Street. Members approved plans for a clubhouse of our own, with an estimated $2,500 needed for materials and masonry, but the project nearly stalled because no suitable building site was within the Post's means.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-olathe-legion-clubhouse-1923" aria-label="Reference 7">[7]</a></sup>
The turning point came when J. D. Cox announced that **R. P. “Bob” Mills**, an Olathe businessman and landowner associated with R. P. Mills & Co., had offered to donate a lot for the building.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-veirs-olathe-mills" aria-label="Reference 12">[12]</a></sup> Members welcomed the offer and formally thanked Mills. With a site available, volunteers contributed construction labor, hauling, and other services to turn the plans into a permanent home for the Post.
The clubhouse was completed and dedicated at a district conference on **January 12–13, 1923**.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-olathe-legion-clubhouse-1923" aria-label="Reference 7">[7]</a></sup>
## Olathe Veterans Memorial
The Olathe Veterans Memorial stands near our Post hall. In 2004, Post 24 members and the Olathe community built the memorial to honor the service and sacrifice of local veterans.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-legion-centennial-post24" aria-label="Reference 2">[2]</a></sup>
