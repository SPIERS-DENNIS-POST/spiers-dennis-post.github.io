# Our history
Spiers-Dennis Post No. 24 has been part of Olathe for more than a century. Our Post was already active here by **1920**.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-coconino-sun-1920" aria-label="Reference 1">[1]</a></sup>
Our Post bears the names of two men connected to Olathe who lost their lives in military service during World War I: **Private Earl C. Spiers, U.S. Army**, and **Seaman John T. Dennis, U.S. Navy**.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-legion-centennial-post24" aria-label="Reference 2">[2]</a></sup>
## The men behind our name
### Earl Christie Spiers — U.S. Army
Earl Christie Spiers was born in Olathe and served as a private in the **109th Infantry Regiment, 28th Division**. He died in France on **September 7, 1918**.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-colorado-freedom-spiers" aria-label="Reference 3">[3]</a></sup><sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-spiers-oise-aisne" aria-label="Reference 4">[4]</a></sup>
During the **Oise-Aisne Offensive**, Spiers's division was fighting in northeastern France near **Fismes**, between the Vesle and Aisne rivers. On September 4, the 28th Division crossed the Vesle and advanced about two miles, taking **Fismette, Courlandon, Baslieux, and Le Grand Hameau**. Over the next three days, American troops continued toward the Aisne under heavy resistance near **Glennes**. They faced German counterattacks, aircraft, machine-gun fire, and artillery bombardments that included **mustard-gas shells**.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-army-oise-aisne-28th" aria-label="Reference 8">[8]</a></sup>
The division was still fighting on **September 7, the day Spiers died**, and was relieved during the night of September 7–8. These Army accounts describe his division's movements and the fighting it faced; Spiers's exact position and the circumstances of his death have not been established.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-army-oise-aisne-28th" aria-label="Reference 8">[8]</a></sup>
Spiers is buried at **Oise-Aisne American Cemetery** in France, Plot D, Row 9, Grave 20.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-spiers-oise-aisne" aria-label="Reference 4">[4]</a></sup>
### John Thomas Dennis — U.S. Navy
John Thomas Dennis was a seaman in the U.S. Navy whose home and family connections were in Olathe. He enlisted at **Des Moines, Iowa, on November 11, 1916**, and later served aboard **USS *Cyclops* (AC-4)**, a Navy collier.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-navy-war-dead-dennis" aria-label="Reference 5">[5]</a></sup><sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-navy-cyclops-assessment" aria-label="Reference 9">[9]</a></sup>
On her final voyage, *Cyclops* was carrying **approximately 10,800 long tons of manganese ore** from Brazil to Baltimore. Before leaving Brazil, the ship had developed a cracked cylinder in her starboard engine, leaving her to proceed under the power of the remaining engine. The heavy ore cargo also raised questions about the ship's stability and structural strength.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-naval-historical-foundation-cyclops" aria-label="Reference 10">[10]</a></sup>
On **March 4, 1918**, *Cyclops* left Barbados for Baltimore with **306 people aboard**, including Dennis. She disappeared without a distress call. **No one aboard is known to have survived**, and no wreck has ever been found.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-navy-lost-all-hands-1918" aria-label="Reference 11">[11]</a></sup> Navy historians have considered whether the dense cargo, engine trouble, and possible structural weaknesses contributed to her loss, but **the cause remains unknown**.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-navy-cyclops-assessment" aria-label="Reference 9">[9]</a></sup><sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-naval-historical-foundation-cyclops" aria-label="Reference 10">[10]</a></sup> The Navy officially recorded Dennis's death as **June 14, 1918**, although that was not a known date of sinking.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-navy-war-dead-dennis" aria-label="Reference 5">[5]</a></sup>
## Our first clubhouse
In 1921, our headquarters and clubrooms occupied a rented house on Main Street. Members wanted a permanent home, and a building project took shape when R. P. Mills offered land for the clubhouse. Post members contributed construction labor and hauling, with help from the wider community.
The new clubhouse was completed and dedicated during a district conference on **January 12–13, 1923**.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-olathe-legion-clubhouse-1923" aria-label="Reference 7">[7]</a></sup>
## Olathe Veterans Memorial
The Olathe Veterans Memorial stands near our Post hall. In 2004, Post 24 members and the Olathe community built the memorial to honor the service and sacrifice of local veterans.<sup class="reference"><a href="#reference-legion-centennial-post24" aria-label="Reference 2">[2]</a></sup>
