# Spiers-Dennis Post No. 24
Source for https://spiers-dennis-post.github.io/, the Jekyll-powered GitHub Pages site of American Legion Post 24 in Olathe, Colorado.
## Architectural principle
**Dumb pages, smart source code.** Put complexity in the source files and build-time logic, not in the pages visitors load. Use Jekyll, Liquid, and structured data to generate simple, semantic, machine-readable HTML and static assets. Favor open standards, accessibility, low bandwidth and processing costs, and reliable rendering across devices and browsers. Avoid unnecessary client-side JavaScript, heavy frameworks, and proprietary dependencies so the site remains cheap and universal to render for people and machines alike.
## Editing
All pages are edited as source files in this repository. The site needs no CMS, database, tracking scripts, or JavaScript to display its content. Preview locally with Ruby and Bundler by running `bundle install` and `bundle exec jekyll serve`.
- `_data/post.yml`: canonical Post name, Post hall's physical address, PO Box mailing address, public desk telephone, and official Post email.
- `_data/officers.yml`: officer assignments and reporting date; the roster appears in `governance/index.md`.
- `_data/sources.yml`: reference records for historical and organizational claims.
- `_data/facts.yml`: reserved structured facts for later publication.
- `_news/` and `_letters/`: dated Markdown content for news and Commander's letters. Their index pages remain unindexed and unlinked from the main navigation until content exists.
- `_includes/`: citations and Schema.org metadata; `_layouts/`: shared HTML.
- `robots.txt` and `sitemap.xml`: crawler discovery.

## Scheduled next-meeting build
`_includes/next-meeting-date.html` computes the next approved fourth-Tuesday meeting at build time for the current or following year, respecting the December exception years in `_data/meeting_calendar.yml`. `_includes/next-meeting.html` displays an ordinary HTML next-meeting section on Home and Meetings. `meetings/next.ics` generates a calendar event from exactly the same date, including at year boundaries. No runtime JavaScript is required.
`.github/workflows/refresh-pages.yml` requests a Pages rebuild through GitHub's Pages API on the morning after the fourth Tuesday and on January 1. Its scheduled workflow checks the weekday on the 23rd through 29th to find that Wednesday. It can also run manually. The native Pages build still runs after ordinary commits to `main`. GitHub may delay scheduled runs or disable schedules in inactive public repositories; review the workflow history periodically. This build strategy relies on the branch-based GitHub Pages configuration and a token with Pages write permission.
## Editorial standards
Write as the Post addressing a visitor. Prefer useful facts, plain language, and direct links. Treat familiar local landmarks and Post activities as part of our own story, not as discoveries made by third-party researchers. Cite historical and formal claims without explaining the research process at length. Do not place source-evaluation notes, confidence scores, alternative property leads, or editing instructions on public pages. Keep those in the repository's research documentation.
If important details such as a meeting time or public opening hours are not confirmed, don't guess. Direct visitors to the Post's shared contact channels rather than displaying rows of empty fields. Preserve old page URLs and remove `noindex: true` when a page has substantive material ready for indexing.
National American Legion eligibility and programs belong on the national website; link instead of copying those rules. The Post's original Constitution and Bylaws have not yet been supplied and must not be replaced with national templates.

## Namesakes research
The namesakes were Pvt. Earl C. Spiers (U.S. Army) and Seaman John Thomas Dennis (U.S. Navy). The Colorado Freedom Memorial identifies Spiers as Earl Christie Spiers and Olathe-born; a transcription of American Battle Monuments Commission burial records documents September 7, 1918, the 109th Infantry Regiment/28th Division, and Oise-Aisne American Cemetery Plot D, Row 9, Grave 20. Do not use the September 7 date to invent a battlefield, cause of death, or Argonne connection; his exact circumstances and original birth date remain unconfirmed from an individual primary record.
The U.S. Navy's official 1920 naval-dead register identifies Dennis as a seaman, not necessarily a signalman; it records his enlistment on November 11, 1916, his assignment to USS Cyclops, and June 14, 1918 as the official death date. Navy historical records place Cyclops's disappearance after the March 4, 1918 Barbados departure. June 14 must not be described as a verified sinking date. The ship's loss and cause are unknown; no verified burial site for Dennis has been identified. The Navy register names a historical foster mother in Olathe, but those family details are not needed in the website's concise public history.
Research source report supplied October 27, 2026. Source links for the published claims are registered in `_data/sources.yml`. The Colorado Freedom Memorial individual profile was supplied by the report but was not independently retrievable during this update; the other listed supporting sources were cross-checked. Original record links should be retained so claims can be revisited.

## Oise-Aisne combat context for Earl Spiers
The official Army Medical Department history of the Oise-Aisne operation places the 28th Division across the Vesle on September 4, 1918. It reports the capture of Fismette, Courlandon, Baslieux and Le Grand Hameau, and fighting toward the Aisne September 5–7 in the face of German resistance near Glennes, counterattacks, aircraft activity and mustard-gas artillery. The division was relieved during the night of September 7–8. This directly places his division in the campaign when his burial record dates his death, but does not establish Spiers's own precise location, company, or manner of death. Do not equate the name Oise-Aisne American Cemetery with proof of an individual battlefield location. Source: https://achh.army.mil/history/book-wwi-fieldoperations-chapter13/

## Cyclops final-voyage research
The USS Cyclops was designated AC-4 (Fuel Ship/Collier No. 4), not AC-6; USS Mars was AC-6. Cyclops's return cargo was approximately 10,800 long tons of manganese ore, not molybdenum. The Naval Historical Foundation's account documents a cracked cylinder in her starboard engine, leaving her running on one shaft, and notes a heavily laden condition. Naval History and Heritage Command considers potential interactions among ore loading, design weaknesses and machinery condition, but no mechanism of loss has been conclusively established. The vessel departed Barbados on March 4, 1918 and disappeared without a distress call or located wreckage. Do not publish an unproven overload or sinking explanation as fact. These details are integrated into John Thomas Dennis's existing biography in `history/index.md`, with references 9 and 10 at the foot of that page.
## Cyclops survivor and casualty accounting
October 27, 2026 research report reviewed and compared with Naval History and Heritage Command accounts. No authenticated survivor of the ship's final voyage, no identified human remains and no confirmed wreck have been recorded. Some sailors who formerly served aboard Cyclops, including watch officer Conrad A. Nervig, survived because they were transferred to other ships before Cyclops disappeared; they did not survive the disappearance itself. An April 15, 1918 Navy announcement initially counted 293 people aboard. A subsequent May 22 report added 13 presumed passengers transferred from other vessels, producing the contemporary total of 306. Current Navy H-Gram historical coverage also uses 306, while some modern articles state 309. The public biography uses the Navy's 306 and the uncontested absence of survivors; avoid burdening it with accounting discrepancies unless primary rosters are reconstructed. Original sources include https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/about-us/leadership/director/directors-corner/h-grams/h-gram-060/h-060-2.html and https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/1969/july/cyclops-mystery. Do not imply Nervig witnessed the ship's disappearance.
## R. P. Mills and the first clubhouse
A February 23, 1923 contemporary newspaper feature describes how the Post approved building plans in fall 1921, with approximately $2,500 estimated for materials and masonry, but could not secure a suitable affordable lot. J. D. Cox announced an offer by local landowner R. P. Mills to donate a site, after which the Post thanked him and carried the project forward through member and community labor. The clubhouse was dedicated on January 12–13, 1923. Its original land deed has not been inspected, so distinguish Mills's documented offer from a confirmed conveyance, and do not assume this historic clubhouse was on the current hall's lot.
Lawrence N. Veirs's Bits and Pieces of Olathe History index lists "Mills, R. P. (Bob)" on pages 4, 19, 24, 28, 44, and 89, and also lists R. P. Mills & Co., Mills General Store, and Zetta (Gaines) Mills. This is useful evidence that the donor was a member of Olathe's established business community, but the full name Robert P. Mills, reported birth details, and identification of his wife remain genealogical leads until verified from original records. An eventual Montrose County deed search for 1921–1923 may identify the specific lot, transaction date, and recipient. The original index PDF was formerly hosted at montrosecogenealogy.org, but the link is now dead and the domain redirects to unrelated content; do not link to it from the public site. The search-indexed transcript retains the Mills entries. A library-held copy of Veirs's history or its index would be preferable for future verification.
## Cyclops March 1918 weather
A January 1970 Proceedings comment reexamined earlier claims of no severe weather and reported a reconstructed front on March 8 across Cyclops's estimated position. The freighter Amalco (occasionally misspelled Amolco) encountered a serious gale on March 9–10, but its captain denied a reported sighting of Cyclops; the latter ship's track after Barbados has never been established. Heavy weather is a plausible contributing factor, not an observed condition aboard Cyclops or a proven cause of her loss. The website mentions the weather briefly within the Dennis biography and cites https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/1970/january/comment-and-discussion. See also https://navyhistory.org/2013/06/unanswered-loss-uss-cyclops-march-1918/ for the denied sighting and uncertainty.

American Legion Centennial material names Oscar D. Roatcap as a Post 24 charter member. History Colorado's article with a 1925 Denver Post baseball tournament team photograph identifies an Oscar Roatcap as the Olathe first baseman and another player R. ## Public Documents aggregation
The Public Documents page at `/documents/` uses Jekyll's `site.pages` at build time to collect approved `documents` declared on individual pages. This is not a central document registry: a document belongs to its primary page, such as Donate, Membership, Post Hall or Governance. Declare documents in that page's YAML front matter. Each record needs a unique `id`, a visitor-facing `title`, a valid `file` URL, a `category` and `public: true`. Optional fields include `date`, `status`, `format` and `description`. The accepted `file` URLs start with `/` for local assets or `https://` for externally hosted records. Each page's eligible records appear automatically at the end of its content through `_layouts/default.html`, immediately before its references. To position them in the text, set `documents_positioned: true` and put `{% include page-documents.html %}` at the desired point, optionally specifying `heading="Download records"`.
The directory loops through `site.pages`, categorizes entries with `_data/document_categories.yml`, and deduplicates by `id` (falling back to `file`), preserving source-page attribution. Unknown categories fall back to Other public documents. No source page needs to list `/documents/` explicitly. To publish an otherwise unplaced document directly in the directory, add its record to the `documents:` front matter of `documents/index.md`; ordinary text links in the Documents page body are also allowed but are not automatically categorized. The initial live examples are the external January 2026 Department of Colorado membership-report PDF owned by the Membership page and the external February 1923 clubhouse newspaper PDF owned directly by Public Documents. Other reference PDFs linked throughout the site are not automatically treated as official Post documents.
Do not assume `public: false` makes a file private: any file committed to this public repository or placed in `assets/documents/` is publicly retrievable whether or not it is indexed. Examine documents for personal information, donor data, privileged content and building security details before uploading. The Post authorized publication of the original one-page IRS determination letter. It is stored as an unaltered PDF at `assets/documents/post24-irs-501c19-determination-2014-06-24.pdf` and is declared under Donate's `documents:` front matter. The Public Documents index collects it automatically; keep the addendum caveat visible. This site generates static HTML with no client-side document loader.
## Build-generated meeting calendar
The Meetings page uses `site.time` and `_includes/meeting-year-dates.html` to calculate fourth Tuesdays for the year in which Jekyll builds the site. This renders ordinary HTML, without runtime JavaScript. December is normally excluded, but `_data/meeting_calendar.yml` lists individually approved December meeting years. For 2026, members voted on October 27 to hold the December 22 meeting. Adding or removing a year in this data file updates the date listing, per-date calendar link, and full-year iCalendar download on the next build. The December-only `meetings/calendar/12.ics` yields a calendar without an event in years without an approved December meeting. Each linked date downloads a provider-neutral one-event iCalendar file generated by `_includes/single-meeting-calendar.ics` and the `meetings/calendar/01.ics` through `meetings/calendar/12.ics` wrappers. All dates and events use America/Denver local time, including the daylight-saving rule, and need no JavaScript or proprietary calendar URL. The Jekyll-processed `meetings/calendar.ics` publishes the same year's scheduled events as a single iCalendar download, using a one-hour editable placeholder for their unknown end times. The `.ics` file and page advance to the new year only after the site is rebuilt; a GitHub Pages redeployment that does not actually rebuild the source will not recalculate the year.
