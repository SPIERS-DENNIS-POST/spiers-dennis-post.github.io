# Spiers-Dennis Post No. 24
Source for https://spiers-dennis-post.github.io/, the Jekyll-powered GitHub Pages site of American Legion Post 24 in Olathe, Colorado.
## Editing
All pages are edited as source files in this repository. The site needs no CMS, database, tracking scripts, or JavaScript to display its content. Preview locally with Ruby and Bundler by running `bundle install` and `bundle exec jekyll serve`.
- `_data/post.yml`: canonical Post name, Post hall's physical address, PO Box mailing address, public desk telephone, and official Post email.
- `_data/officers.yml`: officer assignments and reporting date; the roster appears in `governance/index.md`.
- `_data/sources.yml`: reference records for historical and organizational claims.
- `_data/facts.yml`: reserved structured facts for later publication.
- `_news/` and `_letters/`: dated Markdown content for news and Commander's letters. Their index pages remain unindexed and unlinked from the main navigation until content exists.
- `_includes/`: citations and Schema.org metadata; `_layouts/`: shared HTML.
- `robots.txt` and `sitemap.xml`: crawler discovery.
## Editorial standards
Write as the Post addressing a visitor. Prefer useful facts, plain language, and direct links. Treat familiar local landmarks and Post activities as part of our own story, not as discoveries made by third-party researchers. Cite historical and formal claims without explaining the research process at length. Do not place source-evaluation notes, confidence scores, alternative property leads, or editing instructions on public pages. Keep those in the repository's research documentation.
If important details such as a meeting time or public opening hours are not confirmed, don't guess. Direct visitors to the Post's shared contact channels rather than displaying rows of empty fields. Preserve old page URLs and remove `noindex: true` when a page has substantive material ready for indexing.
National American Legion eligibility and programs belong on the national website; link instead of copying those rules. The Post's original Constitution and Bylaws have not yet been supplied and must not be replaced with national templates.
## Research notes
The original charter date is not established from the Centennial source. The Coconino Sun (September 10, 1920) names the Spiers-Dennis Post of Olathe; a February 23, 1923 newspaper feature describes its rented Main Street headquarters in 1921 and January 12–13, 1923 clubhouse dedication. Accordingly the previous website's "Founded in 2014" cannot be the Post's original founding date, although the exact charter year remains unverified. A corporate-formation date in a secondary state-registry mirror and property information for a differently written street address are not verified as historical charter or hall facts. Previously listed contact numbers, personal email addresses, and secondary-directory officer claims do not supersede the information directly supplied by the Post. Keep these uncertainties out of everyday visitor copy.
The January 30, 2026 Colorado Department membership report records 5 new, 4 PUFL, 53 renewals and 1 headquarters transfer, total 63 and goal 63. This is a dated reporting snapshot, not a live membership roll.

## Meeting schedule
The Post Commander confirmed meetings on the fourth Tuesday monthly, dinner at 5:00 p.m., and usually canceling the December meeting due to holidays. The Commander clarified the meeting starts at 5:30 p.m. Both the meeting page and `_data/facts.yml` now publish that time. Avoid claiming December is canceled every year without a dated announcement.

## Namesakes research
The namesakes were Pvt. Earl C. Spiers (U.S. Army) and Seaman John Thomas Dennis (U.S. Navy). The Colorado Freedom Memorial identifies Spiers as Earl Christie Spiers and Olathe-born; a transcription of American Battle Monuments Commission burial records documents September 7, 1918, the 109th Infantry Regiment/28th Division, and Oise-Aisne American Cemetery Plot D, Row 9, Grave 20. Do not use the September 7 date to invent a battlefield, cause of death, or Argonne connection; his exact circumstances and original birth date remain unconfirmed from an individual primary record.
The U.S. Navy's official 1920 naval-dead register identifies Dennis as a seaman, not necessarily a signalman; it records his enlistment on November 11, 1916, his assignment to USS Cyclops, and June 14, 1918 as the official death date. Navy historical records place Cyclops's disappearance after the March 4, 1918 Barbados departure. June 14 must not be described as a verified sinking date. The ship's loss and cause are unknown; no verified burial site for Dennis has been identified. The Navy register names a historical foster mother in Olathe, but those family details are not needed in the website's concise public history.

## Oise-Aisne combat context for Earl Spiers
The official Army Medical Department history of the Oise-Aisne operation places the 28th Division across the Vesle on September 4, 1918. It reports the capture of Fismette, Courlandon, Baslieux and Le Grand Hameau, and fighting toward the Aisne September 5–7 in the face of German resistance near Glennes, counterattacks, aircraft activity and mustard-gas artillery. The division was relieved during the night of September 7–8. This directly places his division in the campaign when his burial record dates his death, but does not establish Spiers's own precise location, company, or manner of death. Do not equate the name Oise-Aisne American Cemetery with proof of an individual battlefield location. Source: https://achh.army.mil/history/book-wwi-fieldoperations-chapter13/
